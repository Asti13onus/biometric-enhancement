# Fingerprint Dataset Dossier

**Enhancement of fingerprints degraded by occupational wear, ageing and skin damage**
Master's thesis · Astitva Srivastava · compiled 10 September 2026

---

## 1. Purpose and how to read this document

This document is a complete account of the image data available to the thesis. For every
dataset it records: where it comes from, how it is obtained, what it actually contains as
measured on disk, what its quality is, what is wrong with it, who has used it in the
published literature, and what role it plays here.

Three things distinguish it from a dataset list:

1. **Every quantity was measured, not quoted.** Image counts, dimensions, colour modes and
   byte sizes come from per-image manifests generated by `scripts/make_manifest.py` and
   summarised by `scripts/dataset_dossier_stats.py`. Where our measurement disagrees with
   the publisher's specification, both are given and the disagreement is flagged. Two such
   disagreements were found and are material — see §4.1 and §4.6.
2. **Failures are reported as fully as successes.** Section 5 lists what could not be
   obtained and why, because the shape of the thesis is determined at least as much by the
   missing data as by the present data.
3. **Suitability is argued, not asserted.** Section 7 states plainly what this corpus does
   and does not license us to claim.

Live access status is tracked in `docs/datasets/REGISTRY.md`; this dossier is the
point-in-time narrative version of that table.

## 4. Acquired datasets — detail

### 4.1 SOCOFing — Sokoto Coventry Fingerprint Dataset ★ primary damage benchmark

- **Link:** https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/ruizgara/socofing — paper at https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.10609
- **Reference:** Shehu, Ruiz-Garcia, Palade & James, *Sokoto Coventry Fingerprint Dataset*, arXiv:1807.10609, 2018
- **Licence:** CC BY 4.0. Free, instant, no registration. **Redistribution permitted with attribution** — the only acquired real-subject set for which this is true.
- **Status:** downloaded, manifested, 10 September 2026.

**What it is.** 6,000 real fingerprints from 600 African subjects — 10 fingers each, one
impression per finger — plus 49,270 synthetically altered versions of those same prints.
Alterations are of three kinds, each at three severities:

| Alteration types | Easy | Medium | Hard | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Obliteration (`Obl`), Z-cut (`Zcut`), central rotation (`CR`) | 17,931 | 17,067 | 14,272 | **49,270** |

**Verified contents (measured).** 55,270 images, 810.6 MB. 600 subjects. Almost perfectly
balanced across the ten finger positions (5,334–5,790 images each). 54,837 images are
96×103 px; 433 are 241×298. The 49,270 altered images are 8-bit greyscale; the 6,000 real
images are stored RGBA (5,956) or RGB (44) despite greyscale content — **loaders must
convert.**

**Why it matters here — this is the most valuable dataset we hold.** It is the only acquired
set that is simultaneously (a) real fingerprints, (b) paired — every altered image has its
undamaged mate, (c) labelled by damage *type*, (d) labelled by damage *severity*, and
(e) labelled by *gender*. That combination supports the thesis's central experiment
directly: enhance a damaged print, match it against its own clean mate, and report the
change in equal error rate stratified by severity and damage type. No licence, no waiting.

**Quality assessment and caveats — read before using.**

- **The declared 500 dpi is not credible and we do not accept it.** At 96×103 px, 500 dpi
  would describe a finger 4.9 × 5.2 mm across. The true sampling is nearer 200 dpi. Every
  500-dpi-trained model (NFIQ 2, `mindtct`, `pyfing`) assumes 500 dpi and will mis-estimate
  ridge frequency on these images. **Consequence:** images must be rescaled to a common
  effective resolution before use, and NFIQ 2 scores must never be compared between SOCOFing
  and a genuinely 500-dpi set. This is recorded as a hard rule, not a footnote.
- **The alterations are synthetic**, generated by the authors' own software. They model
  obliteration, cuts and rotation — they are *not* a model of occupational wear, ageing or
  dryness. SOCOFing therefore measures robustness to damage; it does not stand in for the
  target population.
- **Gender is imbalanced roughly 4:1** (44,203 male / 11,067 female images). Sufficient for
  a reported stratification, too skewed for a strong claim of demographic fairness.
- One impression per real finger, so genuine pairs exist only between a print and its
  altered versions — not between two independent captures.

**Use in the literature.** Predominantly damage/alteration *classification* work — finger
and gender classification, alteration-type recognition. Its use as an *enhancement*
benchmark is uncommon, which is an opportunity rather than a warning.

### 4.2 FVC2000 / FVC2002 / FVC2004 — "B" subsets ★ the field's common currency

- **Links:** http://bias.csr.unibo.it/fvc2000/download.asp · http://bias.csr.unibo.it/fvc2002/download.asp · http://bias.csr.unibo.it/fvc2004/download.asp
- **Reference:** Maio, Maltoni, Cappelli, Wayman & Jain, FVC2000 / FVC2002 / FVC2004 competition reports
- **Licence:** "B" subsets free for research. The larger "A" subsets ship only with the *Handbook of Fingerprint Recognition*.
- **Status:** all twelve databases downloaded and manifested, 10 September 2026.

**What it is.** Three biennial competitions, four sensor databases each. Each "B" subset is
10 fingers × 8 impressions = 80 images. We hold all twelve: **960 images, 133.9 MB.**
DB1–DB3 are optical, capacitive and thermal-sweep sensors respectively; DB4 is synthetic
(SFinGe). FVC2002 and FVC2004 deliberately instructed subjects to produce *difficult*
impressions — exaggerated distortion, and deliberately wet or dry fingers.

**Verified contents.** 100% 8-bit greyscale. Four distinct image sizes per year, matching
the published specification exactly in every case. Declared dpi is 500 except FVC2002 DB3
(569) and FVC2004 DB3 (512).

**Why it matters here.** FVC2004 DB1 is the single most directly relevant real dataset we
hold: *deliberately dry and distorted fingers* are the closest freely available analogue to
occupational wear. It is our Phase 0 smoke-test set and a headline evaluation set. Just as
importantly, FVC is the most-reported benchmark in the entire enhancement literature, so
results on it are comparable — in kind if not in exact subset — with almost every paper we
cite.

**Quality assessment and caveats.** Genuinely low-quality real data, which is the point.
The limitation is size: 80 images per database gives 10 × C(8,2) = 280 genuine pairs and
2,880 impostor pairs per database — enough for an EER estimate, but with wide confidence
intervals, so **EER must be reported with bootstrap intervals rather than as a point
estimate.** Note also that Cappelli's 2026 state of the art trained on the *A* subsets of
FVC2002 DB2/DB3 and FVC2004 DB1, which we do not hold; we therefore cannot reproduce his
training set exactly, and say so.

**Use in the literature.** The canonical benchmark of the classical era and much of the
deep-learning era: Hong-Wan-Jain evaluation, FingerNet, Cappelli 2023 / 2024a / 2024b / 2026
(training and evaluation), and essentially all quality and segmentation studies.

### 4.3 Anguli-generated paired corpus ★ our training set

- **Link:** https://dsl.cds.iisc.ac.in/projects/Anguli/ (generator) — corpus generated locally
- **Reference:** Ansari, *Generation of Synthetic Fingerprint Databases*, MSc thesis, IISc Bangalore, 2011
- **Licence:** Generator free for research. **The corpus is ours** — we generated it, so it carries no redistribution restriction and can be released with the thesis.
- **Status:** generated and reconciled, 10 September 2026.

**What it is.** 5,584 synthetic fingers, each with one clean master and three simulated
impressions: **22,336 images, 654 MB**, at 275×400 px. Provenance recorded in
`data/processed/anguli_dev_5584/generation.json`.

**Why it matters here.** This is the only source of *perfectly paired* clean/degraded data
we have, and pairing is what supervised enhancement training requires. The master image is
the reconstruction target; our own degradation code produces the input. Because the master
is a clean synthetic ridge pattern, minutiae extracted from it serve as **pseudo ground
truth**, which is how we obtain minutiae precision and recall without owning an
expert-annotated dataset (see §7.2).

**Quality assessment and caveats.**

- Honest disclosure: the run was **requested at 20,000 fingers and interrupted at 5,584**
  when the machine exhausted memory. The directory is named `anguli_dev_5584` rather than
  `anguli_20k` precisely so that no reader mistakes its size. One torn record was detected
  and pruned by `scripts/reconcile_anguli.py`.
- 5,584 is nonetheless ample. Cappelli's 2026 state of the art was trained on **360 images**
  and outperforms FingerGAN trained on 130,000. Corpus size is not this field's bottleneck.
- Synthetic prints are not real prints. Anguli's impression variation models acquisition
  noise, not physiology. **All degradation in this thesis is applied by our own code**
  (`src/fpe/degradation/`), never by Anguli's built-in artefact flags — so that what was
  applied to each image is fully inspectable rather than hidden inside a closed binary. That
  division of labour is a deliberate methodological choice, recorded in ADR 0003.

### 4.4 DTD — Describable Textures Dataset (backgrounds, not fingerprints)

- **Link:** https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/data/dtd/
- **Reference:** Cimpoi, Maji, Kokkinos, Mohamed & Vedaldi, *Describing Textures in the Wild*, CVPR 2014
- **Licence:** Free for research use.
- **Status:** downloaded — 5,640 images, 633 MB.

**What it is and why it is here.** 5,640 texture photographs in 47 categories. Not
fingerprint data — it is the surface-background bank used to composite realistic
latent-style degradation, following the SFP method. It exists to build the **control arm**
of the thesis: a conventional latent-degradation model against which our wear model is
compared.

**Quality note that matters for the thesis's credibility.** Cappelli's principal
methodological criticism of DNUNets and FingerGAN is that they composited training latents
onto backgrounds *extracted from the test images*, leaking test information into training.
We answer that structurally rather than procedurally: DTD images were never fingerprints, so
no fingerprint test image can leak through them, and DTD is partitioned **by category** via
a filename hash into 35 / 8 / 4 categories = 4,200 / 960 / 480 textures. A training image
cannot receive a texture from the same family as a test image, and `split="test"` cannot
return a training texture. Verified disjoint at both category and file level.

### 4.5 MINEX validation imagery

- **Link:** https://github.com/usnistgov/minex
- **Licence:** NIST — public domain, permissive. Redistributable.
- **Status:** downloaded and manifested — **801 images, 114.4 MB, 54 subjects.**

**What it is.** The validation image set shipped with NIST's MINEX III template-conformance
programme. Files are headerless raw `.gray`.

**A finding of our own.** The images ship without metadata, but the validation driver's C++
header carries, for all 801 images, the **width, height, quality band and finger position**.
We parse it (`src/fpe/data/minex.py`), which yields a ready-made stratification axis —
8 finger positions, no thumbs, 54 subjects, **132 images labelled poor or fair** — on
permissively licensed data. Every image has near-unique dimensions (788 distinct sizes
across 801 images), as they are individually cropped.

**Role here.** A secondary real-data evaluation set whose particular value is the
finger-position and quality-band labels, which allow stratified reporting that most
published work omits.

### 4.6 L3-SF — Level-3 Synthetic Fingerprints

- **Link:** https://github.com/luannd/L3-SF
- **Reference:** Wyzykowski, Segundo & Lemes, *Level Three Synthetic Fingerprint Generation*, ICPR 2020
- **Licence:** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 — non-commercial, share-alike.
- **Status:** downloaded and manifested — **8,140 images, 569.3 MB.**

**What it is.** Synthetic fingerprints carrying level-3 detail — sweat pores and scratches.
740 identities, with a pore-annotated subset carrying TSV pore coordinates.

**A correction to the published description.** The dataset is advertised as 1200 dpi. Our
measurement shows the 1200 dpi figure applies to **only 740 of the 8,140 images** — the
512×512 pore-annotated subset. The remaining 7,400 are 320×240, which at 1200 dpi would
describe a finger 6.8 × 5.1 mm across and is therefore impossible. The main set's true
resolution is recorded as **unknown, to be measured from ridge period**, rather than
guessed. All images are stored RGB despite greyscale content.

**Role here.** Secondary. Level-3 pore detail is beyond the 500-dpi civil-capture regime
this thesis targets, so L3-SF is held for a possible ridge-continuity analysis rather than
for training.

### 4.7 Neurotechnology sample databases

- **Link:** https://www.neurotechnology.com/download.html#databases
- **Licence:** Free sample data provided by the vendor for evaluation.
- **Status:** both downloaded and manifested.

| Subset | Images | MB | Size | dpi | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CrossMatch Verifier 300 | 408 | 39.3 | 504×480 | 500 | 51 fingers × 8 |
| U.are.U 4000 | 520 | 62.2 | 326×357 | 512 | 65 fingers × 8 |

**Role and quality.** Clean, consistent, single-sensor optical capture with eight
impressions per finger. Their value is precisely that they are *good* data: eight
impressions per finger gives 51 × C(8,2) = 1,428 and 65 × C(8,2) = 1,820 genuine pairs —
by some margin the largest genuine-pair counts among our real multi-impression sets, and
therefore the tightest EER estimates. They serve as the **clean control condition**: a
method that damages good images is disqualified regardless of how it performs on bad ones.

## 5. Not acquired — and why

Reporting these fully is not an admission of incompleteness. The availability situation in
this field is itself a documented research problem (§6), and the thesis is deliberately
designed so that none of these is load-bearing.

### 5.1 Blocked by a broken provider — ChaLearn LAP Track 3

- **Link:** https://chalearnlap.cvc.uab.cat/dataset/32/description/
- **What it was to be:** 84,000 paired clean/degraded images — the intended primary training set, and the training set of a substantial part of the denoising literature (Mansar; FPD-M-Net).
- **What happened:** the registration system is broken. Sign-up returns HTTP 500; password reset and repeated sign-up across several email addresses all fail. **Assessed as unrecoverable.**
- **Consequence and response:** ADR 0003. We replaced it in full. Anguli supplies the clean masters, and we reimplemented ChaLearn's degradation ourselves. ChaLearn applies **nine** artefact types — blur, brightness, contrast, elastic transform, occlusion, scratches, resolution reduction, rotation, and compositing onto background textures — and all nine are now implemented in `src/fpe/degradation/latent.py`, seeded, with a per-image record of which artefact fired with which parameters. This is arguably a better position than holding the original, because what was applied to each image is inspectable. We do not claim exact comparability: ChaLearn published its artefact *types* but not their parameter distributions.

## 6. The availability crisis — why this matters scientifically

This is worth stating explicitly, because it converts an obstacle into a contribution.

The field's principal benchmark has been unavailable for **nine years**. SD27 was withdrawn
in January 2017 for lacking the documentation NIST requires for distribution. Yet it remains
the evaluation set in papers published in 2024, 2025 and 2026 — including the current state
of the art — because established groups retain legacy copies.

The consequences are concrete: new entrants cannot reproduce published numbers, comparisons
are not independently verifiable, and the reported ranking of methods rests on a dataset no
reviewer can check. The field has begun to respond. The ATVS group at Universidad Autónoma
de Madrid built GCDB from Guardia Civil casework after finding SD27 undistributable; LFIW
was constructed as a replacement; and Wang et al. (2025) moved their evaluation to MOLF and
MUST.

**This thesis follows that line, and the constraint becomes a design principle:** a
reproducible, precision-first, matcher-centric protocol built entirely on **obtainable**
data, released with split files and scripts. A protocol a reader can actually re-run is
worth more than a number on a dataset they cannot have.

## 7. What this corpus does and does not support

Stated plainly, so that no claim in the thesis outruns its evidence.

### 7.1 Supported without further acquisition

| Capability | Evidence base |
|---|---|
| Supervised training of an enhancement model on clean/degraded pairs | Anguli — 5,584 fingers × 4 images — plus our degradation code |
| A controlled comparison of degradation models: our wear model vs. a conventional latent model | Both implemented in `src/fpe/degradation/`; DTD supplies leakage-free backgrounds |
| Matcher EER before vs. after enhancement, on **real** prints | FVC ×12, Neurotech ×2, FVS, MINEX — all multi-impression |
| EER on **real damaged** prints, stratified by damage type and severity | SOCOFing — 49,270 altered images with mates, 3 types × 3 severities |
| NFIQ 2 quality-distribution shift | All sets; NFIQ 2 installed and verified working |
| Stratification by finger position | MINEX (8 positions, labelled) and SOCOFing (10 positions, balanced) |
| Stratification by gender | SOCOFing — imbalanced 4:1, so reportable but not a fairness claim |
| Failure-to-acquire / failure-to-enrol reduction | Any set; measured as the rate at which the extractor returns a usable template |
| Leakage-free training, verifiably | DTD category-level partition; no fingerprint test image can reach training |

### 7.2 Not supported, and how each gap is covered

| Gap | Why | Cover |
|---|---|---|
| **Expert-marked minutiae ground truth on real prints**, so no true minutiae precision / recall / F1 directly comparable to Cappelli's table | Requires SD27 (withdrawn), or MUST or MOLF (licence not yet granted) | Minutiae precision and recall are computed against **pseudo ground truth** from clean Anguli masters and reported as such — never presented as comparable to published SD27 numbers. Real-data claims rest on **matcher EER**, which requires no annotation. MUST is being pursued in parallel. |
| **A genuinely occupationally worn population** | Rural Indian DB not yet licensed | CASIA-V5 (workers, waiters) in registration; FVC2004 DB1 (deliberately dry and distorted) held now. The wear model's realism is validated by measurement — NFIQ 2 distribution comparison and a domain classifier — rather than asserted. |
| **Longitudinal ageing data** | CASIA Subject Ageing awaiting approval | Ageing enters the wear model as a parameterised physiological effect. The empirical ageing claim is deferred unless the dataset arrives. |
| **Manual orientation / frequency / segmentation ground truth** | Tsinghua and Thai et al. packages not yet requested | We use Cappelli's *pretrained* networks via `pyfing` for orientation, frequency and segmentation rather than retraining them, inheriting his supervision without needing his labels. |
| **Direct numerical comparison with the 2026 state of the art** | SNFEN's published numbers are on SD27, which we cannot obtain | We run `pyfing`'s released GBFEN and SNFEN *ourselves*, on *our* benchmark. A comparison we execute on obtainable data is stronger than a number copied from a table on a dataset nobody can check. |

## 9. Provenance and reproducibility

- Every acquired dataset has a per-image manifest under `docs/datasets/manifests/` recording relative path, **SHA-256**, byte size, pixel dimensions, declared dpi and colour mode. Manifests are committed; **images never are.**
- The per-dataset figures in this document are produced by `scripts/dataset_dossier_stats.py` from those manifests into `docs/datasets/manifest_summary.csv`. Re-running the script regenerates them.
- Downloads are performed by `scripts/download_group_a.py` and `scripts/download_group_b.py`, which verify checksums, cache to avoid re-fetching, and refuse to overwrite existing files.
- Live access status is maintained in `docs/datasets/REGISTRY.md`.
- **Licence discipline.** IAB, NIST, CASIA, FVC-A and LivDet data may not be redistributed. `.gitignore` blocks image formats by default and is never overridden for dataset images. SOCOFing (CC BY 4.0), MINEX (public domain) and our own Anguli corpus are the only holdings that could be redistributed, and only SOCOFing and the Anguli corpus are planned for release alongside the thesis.
